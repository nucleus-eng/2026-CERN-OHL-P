# Experimental Methods

For the biological system, the dataset tracks 16 specific features:
*   **NEB in PMix (binary):** Indicates whether the protein mixture (PMix) was sourced commercially from the New England Biolabs (NEB) kit or prepared in-house.
*   **[PMix] (mg/mL):** The concentration of the protein mixture, which supplies the essential enzymes required to drive transcription, translation, and energy regeneration (such as T7 RNA polymerase, translation factors, and aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases).
*   **[Ribosome] ($\mu$M):** The concentration of 70S ribosomes, which constitute the core ribosomal machinery responsible for translating mRNA into polypeptides.
*   **[tRNA] ($\mu$g/$\mu$L):** The concentration of the total transfer RNA mixture, which is responsible for providing codon-matching amino acid delivery to the ribosome during translation.
*   **[DNA] (nM):** The concentration of the DNA template (e.g., encoding GFP) that serves as the genetic blueprint for synthesizing mRNA via transcription.
*   **[Magnesium acetate] (mM):** The source of free magnesium ions (Mg$^{2+}$), a critical catalytic cofactor essential for the proper function of ribosomes, tRNA synthetases, ATP, and polymerases. Magnesium must be precisely balanced, as excessive chelation or accumulation of phosphate can lead to systemic precipitation and system failure.
*   **[Creatine phosphate] (mM):** The primary phosphagen energy donor that works synergistically with Creatine Kinase to regenerate ATP. While it acts as the primary fuel source, its consumption generates inorganic phosphate (Pi) as a byproduct, which can inhibit the reaction by sequestering free magnesium.
*   **[Potassium glutamate] (mM):** A salt used to provide potassium ions (K$^+$), which mimics the natural cytosolic ionic environment of a living cell to help stabilize the system.
*   **[ATP] (mM):** Adenosine Triphosphate serves as the primary bulk energy currency for transcription and tRNA charging. It also acts as a strong Mg$^{2+}$ chelator and a potential chemical hydrotrope.
*   **[GTP] (mM):** Guanosine Triphosphate is an energy source specifically required to drive the mechanical translocation of the ribosome via elongation factors, a function that cannot be substituted by ATP.
*   **[Amino acid mix] (mM):** A mixture of the 20 standard amino acids, which serve as the fundamental building blocks assembled by the ribosome to synthesize proteins.
*   **[HEPES] (mM):** A chemical buffer used to maintain the reaction's pH stability, typically keeping the environment between a pH of 7.5 and 8.0.
*   **[PPK] ($\mu$M):** Polyphosphate Kinase, a supplemental single-enzyme metabolic energy module that can be added to the baseline PURE system to drive the robust regeneration of nucleoside triphosphates.
*   **[PolyP] (mM):** Polyphosphate, which serves as the specific chemical substrate and fuel source consumed by the PPK module to phosphorylate AMP, ADP, and GDP back into usable energy.
*   **[RNase Inhib] (U/mL):** Ribonuclease inhibitor, added to the mixture to protect the template and synthesized mRNA from degradation by rogue nucleases.
*   **[PEG] (%):** Polyethylene glycol, a polymer used as a macromolecular crowding agent. It mimics the highly dense environment of a living cell to increase the effective concentration of biomolecules and boost transcription, though high concentrations risk protein aggregation.

To capture the experimental context, the system tracks 5 control features:
*   **Reader Type (categorical):** Specifies the hardware instrument used for data acquisition. In the automated b.next pipeline, this typically refers to the specific temperature-controlled plate reader (incubated at 37°C) used to monitor the reactions. Because different plate readers measure native fluorescence in arbitrary units, tracking the specific hardware is critical for properly normalizing the raw Relative Fluorescence Units (RFU) into comparable data.
*   **Gain (categorical):** Indicates the detector sensitivity setting used by the plate reader during the fluorescence scan. Proper gain calibration is essential to ensure the signal remains within the instrument's linear dynamic range without saturating the detector. This is a critical prerequisite for accurately converting raw RFU into absolute protein concentrations (e.g., ng/$\mu$L of GFP) using standardized calibration curves.
*   **Read Type (categorical):** Defines the specific measurement methodology and modality. Crucially, this specifies whether the data is an "endpoint" read (a single snapshot at the end of the reaction) or a "kinetic" (time series) read. 
*   **Product (categorical):** Identifies the specific reporter molecule being synthesized and quantified. For these optimization runs, the product is usually a specific variant of Green Fluorescent Protein, such as "deGFP" (destabilized enhanced GFP) or "plamGFP", each of which possesses distinct maturation speeds and brightness profiles.
*   **Condition (categorical):** Defines the specific group and hardware the formulation was run on. MFG stands for Manufacturing, R&D represents the Research and Development group, and LabCraft designates the platform team whose system IGOR utilizes for its automated iterations.
**Outputs, Design Matrix, and Optimization Goals**
The target outputs comprise a set of sigmoidal fit parameters derived from fluorescence curves, which characterize the yield (steady-state fluorescence), rate, time offset, and a binary success parameter (defined as 1 if PURE synthesis was successful, and 0 if synthesis approximately failed). Detailed descriptions of these fitting methodologies can be found elsewhere [1](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/Bhasin-20260421), [2](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/Newman-20260421).

The final design matrix ingested by IGOR consists of 21 input features (15 real-valued, 1 binary, and 5 categorical) mapped to 4 outputs. An example of an IGOR-ingestible design matrix from iteration-2 is located [here](experiments/20260417-iteration-2-data_tight-bounds-with-ppk.csv)). With the design matrix structured for ingestion, we explicitly define the feature bounds and constraints. For real-valued columns, bounds are established as explicit minimum and maximum values; for categorical columns, bounds are locked to specific discrete values (e.g., `LabCraft`). Currently, the PURE system uses no non-axis-aligned constraints, though IGOR natively supports complex non-axis-aligned constraints (e.g., defining `[ATP] (mM) = 0.5 * [GTP] (mM)`).

For the IGOR CDK iterations, we employ a standardized configuration coined the LabCraft Automation Biological Setup (LABS). Because the Bayesian engine can leverage historical experiments run by parallel b.next groups (e.g., MFG and R&D), the LABS configuration enforces bounds to be defined as in the column `new forced setpoint(s)` of {ref}`table:environment-sequence`. Generally, these bounds are dictated by physical limits (e.g., concentration minimums cannot drop below zero) and reagent availability (e.g., maximum PMix ratio is 1.8 mg/mL). Throughout this project, we navigate three distinct bounding regions: 
1. `tight_bounds`: Restricted by immediate laboratory hardware reagent availability and mixtures readily available in the lab.
2. `dataset_bounds`: Anchored to the absolute minimum and maximum values present in the historical dataset.
3. `loose_bounds`: Broad ranges defined primarily by physical limits, spanning a massive search space.

Finally, the optimization goals are explicitly declared. For these initial iterations, the singular objective was to maximize the yield (the sigmoidal steady-state value). With a fully populated design matrix, correctly embedded features, explicitly defined bounds, and a clear optimization goal, the automated IGOR iteration cycle begins.
